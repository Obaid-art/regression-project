Machine Learning: Regression

Regression Workshop

Khalifa University  ·  Department of Chemical Engineering  ·  Prof. Ali Elkamel

Your group has a real piece of equipment and thirty-one measurements from it. Clean them, split them, teach a line and then a network, and find out which one you would trust with the plant. Ninety minutes. Nothing to install, nothing to sign in to.

Step 0

Which group are you?

Eleven rigs, one per group of three. New to this? Start with the kettle: a practice run where the page shows you what to do, what not to do, and what a good answer looks like. Then pick your real group. Some questions along the way have no right or wrong answer: answer from your own understanding. The page notes the time you complete each step; your report carries that timeline.

Step 2

Split, then standardise

Every fourth kept row is sealed as the test set: no model sees it until the exam. Then the bank teller: x′ = (x − mean) / std, with the mean and std of the training rows only.

Step 2b · Why standardising is a must

The same network, the same gradient descent, the same 300 epochs, twice: on the raw numbers and on the standardised ones.

Step 4

The network board: play

Five knobs, and no limit on how many times you train. Every run lands on the board with its training score and its test score. Two challenges: try to make it memorise (training far above test; not every dataset lets you), and find the smallest network that keeps your best test score. Then pick one, and say why.

Step 5

Who wins on the sealed rows?

Three candidates: the line, your pick from the board, and, where one exists, the physical law with one or two numbers fitted by least squares. All judged on the same sealed rows, the only rows none of them has seen. First, your guess.
